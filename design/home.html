    The instrument reads the market

    One sigil, four readings.

    Favorablestrength 71 · rising
    Waitstrength 44 · two-way
    Unclearstrength 50 · flat, fogged
    Reduce riskstrength 78 · falling

    On Sign-in the sigil is a promise. On Home it becomes an instrument, and every part of it encodes the market read:

    Arc length is trend strength (40° plus 1.2° per point). Arc colour is the stance: amber to coral when favorable, pale gold to amber for Wait, grey and dashed when unclear, rose to crimson to reduce risk. The price line follows the bias into the arc's tip: it rises when bullish, chops sideways when mixed, lies flat when neutral, and falls from above into the tip when bearish. Pace is urgency: the tick ring turns once every 300s when favorable, 150s when reducing risk, and almost stops (600s) when unclear, when the whole instrument also fogs slightly.

    So a glance at the panel gives the mood before a word is read, and the sigil never shows anything the read doesn't say.

    The sigil is generated as vector SVG from the read itself (stance, strength, bias), so there are no image files per state and it stays sharp on any display. The same generator draws the rings that rise behind the navigation (anchored to the window's bottom-left corner, where the sidebar holds no data) and the welcome banner.

    Where the accents go

    A strict budget so the terminal stays a terminal.

    hero · 1 per screenMarket read carries the sigil and the live border

    A thin light travels around the panel edge in the stance colour with a coral tip. It moves at 16s per lap when favorable, 11s when the answer is Wait, 9s when it is time to reduce risk, and slows to 30s at 45% strength when the read is unclear. It is the only animated border in the app.

    selectionA selected setup takes the colour of its bias

    A soft tint moves in from the left, the monogram glows, and the detail pane gets a matching glow in its corner. Green for bullish, rose for bearish, grey for conditional. This is a tint, not a coloured bar on the edge.

    cautionThe caution well is amber-tinted

    The one sentence every read must carry is tinted amber, so the warning is the second thing the eye finds after the verdict.

    changeRegime shifts are marked in the new regime's colour

    The shift event in Changes is tinted with the regime it moved to. The “Now” edge of the Today ribbon blinks slowly, and when the market state changes, events slide in one by one.

    motionSlow, and only where something is live

    The sigil's tick ring and nodes turn slowly (150–600s per turn) and its tip breathes, the status dot pulses, and the verdict eases in when the read changes. Nothing moves on hover except colour. With reduced motion on, all of it stops and the border arc stays as a static accent.

    neverNo colour behind data

    The sigil sits behind the regime column under a scrim, and its labels carry a dark halo. The stats row and the “Why this read” bar sit on frosted glass. Setups, Changes and the floor stay on solid panels.

    Adapting Raycast to a terminal

    What was adopted, and what was deliberately not.

    Adopted: the void canvas and surface steps, the keycap inset stack, Mist primary buttons, 8/16px radii, Inter at regular weight for large text, Geist Mono for technical metadata, the pipe-separated meta strip, the single coral brand mark, and visible keyboard shortcuts that actually work.

    Not adopted in the app: the cinematic red/blue hero gradient, 56–64px headlines, the 16px body size and the 80–120px section gaps. Those belong to a marketing page. A terminal needs 13–15px text and 8px gaps. The hero treatment can be used on the login screen and a future website.

    Adjusted: the full-strength 25% white ring is kept for small interactive keys (buttons, kbd, selected rows). Large panels use an 8% ring (--key-soft), because six bright outlines would turn the page into card soup. Raycast has no red for losses, so sell is #f0506e, a crimson-rose kept visibly apart from coral. It always comes with a sign or arrow.

    A decision for you: Raycast is dark-only, and this design follows that. The app currently has a light/dark toggle. Retiring light mode is a product call, and DESIGN.md records it as recommended, not done.

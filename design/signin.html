The line

Three ways to say what the sigil means.

B · Illusions (recommended): “Markets are full of illusions. Dispel them before you trade.” The brand name becomes the verb, the market is the subject, and trading is the payoff. It explains the name without a tagline, and it keeps the promise of the old line (see clearly first, trade second).

A · Definition: keeps “See the market clearly before you trade it.” and adds a dictionary entry above it in mono: dis·pel /dɪˈspel/ a spell that clears illusion. It's the gentlest option. The philosophy reads like a footnote to the name.

C · Noise: “Dispel the noise. Trade what's real.” It's the shortest and punchiest, but “noise” is a word every trading app uses. Illusion is the word only Dispel can own.

Why a sigil

Dispel: a spell that clears illusion.

The candles were literal: they said “trading” loudly, and every placement made them compete with the form. The new artwork starts from the name. To dispel is to break an illusion, and Dispel does that with measurement. So the image is a sigil built like a scientific instrument: graduated rings like an astrolabe, orbits, and one coral arc, the spell, whose tip glows softly.

A price line rises from the lower left and ends exactly at the tip of the arc: the moment the spell touches the market. It is the only trading reference, quiet enough to be found rather than announced.

Everything is hairline (1px at 6–28% white) except the coral arc and one coral node. The sigil is centred on the sign-in box and dissolves into the black at its edges, so the page reads as one surface: no panel, no background image.

A or B

Same idea, two hands.

A · Astrolabe is precise and architectural: rings, ticks, diamond nodes (echoing the logo) and slow counter-rotations. It feels like an instrument.

B · Harmonograph is organic: interference curves drawn by two damped pendulums, like a spell written in light. It is pure maths (no randomness) and still.

Both are generated as vector SVG, so they can ship as a small asset file and stay sharp on any display. Motion is limited to A: the tick ring and the diamond nodes turn slowly (300s and 200s per turn) and the tip breathes. The arc and the line stay still, so nothing ever sweeps across text. All of it stops under reduced motion.
